Anomaly Detection in Industrial Process Data

Analysis and evaluation of machine-learning models for anomaly detection in industrial time-series data, using walking beam furnaces (Stoßöfen) as an example.

Course
Vertiefung Berufsfeld – Tun/Forschen/Gründen
Date
16 August 2026
Team
Laila Gruber · Hubert Essletzbichler

Project Overview

The aim is to identify suitable machine-learning methods for anomaly detection in real, multivariate and predominantly unlabeled process data. The work is a criteria-based comparison rather than the promotion of a single algorithm.

Problem Statement

The industrial challenge is anomaly detection in a walking beam furnace process with:

  • more than 100 relevant process signals
  • several process phases: heating, holding and cooling
  • rare and predominantly unlabeled anomalies
  • high requirements for robustness and traceability

Objective

The objective is to evaluate which machine-learning approaches are suitable as a starting point for practical anomaly detection on this furnace process. Suitability is assessed not only by detection behaviour, but also by robustness, interpretability, handling of unlabeled data, and effort required for parameterization.

Data

The study uses industrial process time series from walking beam furnaces. According to the project presentation, more than 100 relevant process signals are involved, covering heating, holding and cooling phases. Anomalies are rare and predominantly unlabeled.

Temperature-, gas- and air-related process quantities form the multivariate time-series context of the use case.

Methodology

The approach moves from process data to a criteria-based evaluation. What mattered was not only the model itself, but also data preparation, the anomaly score and the threshold.

The examined methods are Moving Average, PCA, One-Class SVM, Isolation Forest, Local Outlier Factor and Autoencoder.

Models

  1. Moving Average

    A simple statistical baseline that highlights deviations from a smoothed local level in the time series.

  2. PCA

    Principal Component Analysis used to represent multivariate variation and to flag observations that are poorly explained by the dominant components.

  3. One-Class SVM

    A boundary-based one-class method that models a region of normal operation in feature space.

  4. Isolation Forest

    An ensemble method that isolates unusual observations through random partitions, without requiring labeled anomalies.

  5. Local Outlier Factor

    A density-based method that compares the local neighbourhood of an observation with that of its neighbours.

  6. Autoencoder

    A neural reconstruction model intended for more complex, possibly nonlinear relationships in the process data.

Evaluation Criteria

The methods were considered with respect to, among other aspects:

  • handling of unlabeled data
  • handling of unknown anomaly types
  • robustness
  • interpretability
  • multivariate processing
  • parameterization effort
  • extensibility

Results

For the walking beam furnace process under study, Isolation Forest is the most suitable first model for practical use. One-Class SVM serves as a complementary comparison model. The Autoencoder is a capable but more demanding expansion path.

Preferred model

Isolation Forest

Suitable for unlabeled data, efficient and robust, with a good balance of effort and benefit. Prioritized as the first practically deployable model.

Complementary model

One-Class SVM

Learns the normal operating region and can detect unknown deviations. Requires higher parameterization effort.

Further approach

Autoencoder

Suitable for complex patterns and nonlinear relationships, with higher training and interpretation effort.

Team contribution

  • literature and model research
  • deriving requirements from the use case
  • development of a qualitative evaluation methodology
  • comparison and prioritization of the most suitable approaches

Learnings

  • Anomaly detection depends strongly on data quality, features and thresholds.
  • Interpretable models can be especially valuable at the start.
  • Model choice must always fit the concrete industrial use case.

Next step

Prototypical implementation of Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM and Autoencoder with a unified data basis, comparable preprocessing and a shared evaluation logic.

Conclusion

The project shows how a real industrial problem can be turned into a traceable, criteria-based machine-learning approach. The most complex model is not automatically the most suitable one for industrial use. Fitness depends in particular on the available process data, the requirements of the use case, the planned area of application, interpretability, robustness and parameterization effort.
